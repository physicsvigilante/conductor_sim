# M16a — True elapsed-time, one-dimensional electrostatic motion

## Purpose and relationship to M13–M15

M13b/M15b **position sweeps** evaluate independent equilibria for a selected
charge at a user-authored sequence of locations. Those viewers do **not**
simulate motion. M16a adds an actual classical initial-value problem:

\[
\frac{ds}{dt}=v, \qquad m\frac{dv}{dt}=\mathbf F(\mathbf r_0+s\hat{\mathbf n})\cdot\hat{\mathbf n}.
\]

The force is read from the **existing** `force_energy(solution)` provider
at each integrator evaluation. Its direct self-field exclusion, Kelvin
images, and conductor-background decomposition are retained. Neither a
new electrostatic solver nor a FEMM force approximation is introduced.

```python
from conductor_sim import (
    ElectrostaticProblem, PointCharge, ConductingPlane, Grounded,
    GroundedPlaneBackend, Motion1DSpec, integrate_motion_1d,
)

problem = ElectrostaticProblem(
    (PointCharge(1e-9, (0, 0, .1)),),
    (ConductingPlane(constraint=Grounded()),),
)
spec = Motion1DSpec(
    problem=problem, charge_index=0, mass_kg=1e-6,
    axis=(0, 0, 1), initial_velocity_m_s=0.,
    duration_s=.15, sample_interval_s=.01,
)
trajectory = integrate_motion_1d(spec, GroundedPlaneBackend())
print(trajectory.termination, trajectory.samples[-1].velocity_m_s)
```

`axis` defines the **positive sign** of the velocity and displacement; it
is NOT a rod, track, or constraint force. Motion is allowed only where the
full electrostatic force is provably parallel to that axis.

## Supported physical symmetry and honest refusal

M16a deliberately accepts **one nonzero moving point charge** without fixed
additional point charges, with the following analytical configurations:

- A charge moving along the surface normal of one grounded conducting plane.
- A charge moving normal to two facing parallel infinite conducting planes,
  with grounded/fixed-potential constraints (finite reciprocal image order).
- A charge moving radially outside one grounded or prescribed-net-charge
  solid conducting sphere.
- A charge moving on a line through the centre of one or several concentric
  conducting spherical shells, strictly inside the innermost cavity. A
  centred source is allowed, and the particle may cross the cavity centre.

The authored geometry establishes an invariant line; transverse forces
are also checked at **every** RHS evaluation. Any numerical or custom
provider with an unmodelled transverse force is rejected, never silently
projected onto the line. The chosen backend must explicitly support each
state and supply a valid `ForceEnergyResult`. The existing FEMM backends do
**not** provide numerical forces and are refused rather than substituted with
analytical values. Invalid mass/axis/times, more than one physical point
charge, eccentric shells, off-axis directions, metal starts and unsupported
conductor shapes are rejected before integration or at the first evaluation.

## Numerical method, energy and event semantics

The initial-value problem uses **adaptive fourth-order Runge–Kutta with
step doubling**. A complete step is compared with two half steps, and the
integrator adjusts the actual step size independently of the requested output
timestamps. The event detector locates zero-velocity turning points within
accepted steps without reflecting the particle or stopping at a mere turning
point. Uniform-acceleration test doubles check both displacement and the
turning-time root against independent exact kinematics.

Because ideal image-charge forces become singular near metal, the model has
a positive **surface guard** (default `1e-6` times the characteristic
geometry size; configurable via `surface_guard_m`). If the particle reaches
this threshold, the integration ends with `termination='surface-guard'`, a
recorded last vacuum-side state and a **surface-approach event**. The reported
time is **not** a claim about exact physical collision or a real finite-sized
charged particle, contact, reflection, energy loss or resistance. Every RHS
and all saved samples stay outside the guard.

Each sample stores elapsed seconds, 3D position on the proved line,
signed scalar velocity/acceleration, total axial force, kinetic energy,
mechanical interaction energy, and their sum. `energy_drift_j` is computed
**componentwise against the initial state** before combining with kinetic
energy, avoiding loss of small work under a large *other* constant component.
The absolute total-energy field may still round under extreme backgrounds;
individual component differences and the drift field retain the available
precision. This is not infinite-precision arithmetic. The returned JSON
identifies the results as actual elapsed-time dynamics and does **not** use
M13b's position-sweep playback schema.

For time-independent, fixed-charge/geometry/voltage electrostatic problems,
mechanical energy is conserved in these supported systems. The code reports
the measured drift, rather than silently forcing velocities to agree with
energy conservation.

## Reproduce the CLI and JEE-scale electron example

```bash
python examples/particle_motion_1d.py --case plane --output output/m16-plane.json
python examples/particle_motion_1d.py --case cavity --output output/m16-cavity.json
python examples/particle_motion_1d.py --case sphere-exterior --output output/m16-sphere.json
python examples/particle_motion_1d.py --case plates --output output/m16-plates.json

# An electron released from the centre of 20-V, 20-cm biased plates.
python examples/particle_motion_1d.py --case plates \
  --charge-c -1.602176634e-19 --mass-kg 9.1093837139e-31 \
  --duration-s 5e-8 --sample-interval-s 5e-9 \
  --output output/m16-electron.json
```

The electron example retains image-induced effects from the existing plate
backend. Because a single elementary charge produces negligible induction
for these dimensions, the trajectory independently approaches
`z = z0 + q E t^2/(2m)` (with the correct charge/field signs).
The illustration uses classical, nonrelativistic dynamics; the engine rejects initial or evolving speeds at or above **0.1c**, rather than
silently applying Newtonian dynamics in the relativistic regime. This bound
is a modelling cutoff, not a detailed relativistic solver.

## Acceptance and remaining scope

Tests cover exact constant-acceleration and turning-time references,
nanosecond/electron motion between biased plates, induced-force and energy
identities for a grounded plane and spherical cavity, exterior-sphere and
nested-shell integration, axis reversal, energy-drift sampling, resource
closure, surface-approach events, and strict geometry and transverse-force
refusals. Retained Python 3.10/3.12 and pinned real-FEMM regressions must
stay green before merging; FEMM tests show **non-regression only**.

**Not yet M16b:** no HTML time slider, animated particle, time-based force
and energy plots, collision or bounce animation, browser ODE integration.
**Not yet general dynamics:** no unconstrained 2D/3D paths, other moving or
fixed point charges, changing electrical connections or voltage sources,
magnetic fields, drag/gravity forces, relativistic treatment or moving
conductors. These require explicit later extensions and validation.
