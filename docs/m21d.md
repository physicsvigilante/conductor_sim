# M21d — Far-separated connected spherical conductors

## JEE relevance and source fidelity

The leading self-capacitance model now handles separated conducting spheres
and empty spherical metal shells without pretending they are concentric.
The [acceptance charter](m21d_charter.md) was committed before implementation.

Four source anchors drive the examples:

| Example | Source | Preserved facts / disclosed choices |
|---|---|---|
| `jee2011` | Official IIT-JEE 2011 Paper 1 Q31, physical PDF p13; workbook p16 Multiple Q1 | Larger empty shell A, smaller solid B, initially equal +Q, far apart. Numerical radii, Q, distance and shell thickness are illustrative. |
| `notes-sharing` | `POTENTIAL_CONDUCTORS1.pdf` p68 Illustration 4 | Exact given radii 0.10/0.20 m and +0.5/+0.7 **mC**; expected final +0.4/+0.8 mC. Distance 20 m and 1% walls are illustrative. |
| `equal-density` | Same notes p68 Illustration 5 | R and 2R with equal initial sigma; larger final sigma=5sigma/6. Numerical scale is illustrative. |
| `surface-ratio` | `02_conductors_half_size.pdf` p2 Single Q15 | E1/E2=R2/R1 after connection; numerical radii/initial charges/distance illustrative. |

A fifth `signed-groups` example is explicitly a teacher hypothetical: signed
charge cancellation in one group and a third, unlinked conductor. It is also
a warning case: near-zero leading terms do not carry relative-accuracy claims.

The original source pages were visually inspected; both repository PDF hashes
are verified on export. The 2011 provenance and all four correct relations
were checked against the original official archive, not just the workbook:
https://jeeadv.ac.in/past_qps/2011_1.pdf (physical PDF p13, Q31, answer ABCD).

## Explicitly approximate physics

For homogeneous permittivity epsilon, use each body's *isolated* capacitance
Ci=4*pi*epsilon*Ri, where Ri is its outer radius. For a floating linked group G:

    VG = sum(Qi_initial for i in G) / sum(Ci for i in G)
    Qi_final = Ci * VG
    Vi = Qi/Ci
    sigma_i = Qi/(4*pi*Ri^2)
    E_i,outward = Vi/Ri
    U_self = sum(Qi^2/(2*Ci))

This is the leading far-separated limit: it omits mutual potential, surface
polarization and the wire's capacitance. The interior of each conductor and
its empty closed cavity has zero E. It is **not** an exact finite-separation
boundary solution, a spatial field provider, or a full capacitance matrix.

Admission requires 2–8 external, disjoint SolidSphere/SphericalShell objects,
no point charges anywhere, initially FixedNetCharge, floating named links
with disjoint groups, and max_i sum_(j!=i)(Ri/dij) <= 0.05. That threshold is
an engineering admission policy, **not a proved 5% physical-error bound**.
Poorly resolved coordinates and nonfinite arithmetic are rejected too.

Before and after records expose the omitted centre-monopole potential
estimates k*sum(Qj/dij) and the absolute pair-energy scale
sum_(i<j)|k*Qi*Qj/dij|. These are diagnostic estimates, not exact errors, and
are deliberately **not added** to the self-only solution or energy. Large
charge contrasts, neutral conductors and cancelled group totals can make
relative errors in small leading quantities large even at accepted geometry.
Wire capacitance is assumed negligible; no wire-size accuracy bound is supplied.

Finite-wire current, resistance, discharge time, heating rate, radiative
losses, external sources, dielectric interfaces, grounded groups, voltage
sources, nested/touching/nearby spheres and close-range induction are excluded.
A homogeneous permittivity parameter is supported; material interfaces are not.
No unqualified fallback to the existing concentric-shell/FEMM backends occurs.

The radius-weighted charge-sharing relation and its assumption are explained
in OpenStax University Physics Vol. 2, section 7.5:
https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-2/pages/7-5-equipotential-surfaces-and-conductors
This is a physics reference, not an implementation certification.

## Composition and numerical energy

`conductor_sim.distant_spheres.DistantSphereConnectionBackend` reuses the
existing immutable `ConnectionPlan`, `ElectricalLink`, `ElectrostaticProblem`,
`SolidSphere`, `SphericalShell`, and `ConductorTransfer`. Calling `solve` requires
`approximation='self-capacitance-leading-order'`; approximation selection is
never inferred from geometry or a solver failure. Conductor names are stable
identities. Linked groups conserve their own signed total charge; unlinked
conductors retain charge. Input order, parent model, geometry and identities
remain unchanged. The immutable `resolved_problem` can seed a new connection
plan after an explicitly idealized disconnect/reconnect operation.

Released energy is evaluated as a nonnegative weighted variance:

    U_release = sum_groups sum_i ((Qi_initial - Qi_final)^2 / (2*Ci))

This avoids losing a small release by subtracting very large absolute energies.
A separate consistency check compares the energy totals within floating-point
resolution. The release is available electrostatic energy on settling, not
a guarantee that all of it becomes wire heat. The numerical millicoulomb case
has very high ideal fields: breakdown, leakage and material failure are not modeled.

## Teaching display and reproducibility

The new, self-contained offline display shows two **static** states, solid
versus hollow shapes, per-object Q/V/sigma/E tables, signed transfers and
energy before/after. Radii are proportional; centre spacing is compressed
schematically. Multiple-body diagrams scroll locally instead of overlapping
labels. A label toggle shows stored charge changes, not an animated current.
Source provenance and extra numerical choices remain visible. HTML is
recomputed from the validated immutable result before export; changed result
records are rejected. No older canvas renderer is modified.
