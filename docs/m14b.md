# M14b — Solid conducting sphere inside concentric conducting shells

## Reuse and supported scope

M14b extends the accepted M14a `ConnectionPlan` and `ElectricalLink` authoring
contract to the mixed radial configuration of **one solid conducting sphere as
the innermost object, surrounded by 1–7 strictly separated concentric
`SphericalShell` conductors**. The existing shell-only M14a path is preserved
unchanged. Conductors may individually start grounded or have prescribed net
charge. An ideal floating link redistributes a group's total charge while
preserving the sum; a grounded link holds all connected members at zero
potential, with permitted charge exchange with Earth. Multiple disjoint links,
including nonadjacent solid–outer-shell connections, are supported.

No point sources are allowed in this new *solid+shell* domain. In particular,
external/off-centre point charges or charges in inter-shell gaps destroy the
radial symmetry; we **reject** them rather than invent a concentric source.
Existing all-shell problems with arbitrary sources in the innermost cavity
continue using `NestedShellBackend` without modification.

```python
from conductor_sim import (SolidSphere, SphericalShell, Grounded,
    FixedNetCharge, ElectrostaticProblem, ElectricalLink, ConnectionPlan,
    ConnectedShellBackend)

initial = ElectrostaticProblem(conductors=(
    SolidSphere(.10, FixedNetCharge(20e-6), name='sphere'),
    SphericalShell(.199999, .20, FixedNetCharge(0), name='outer'),
))
plan = ConnectionPlan(initial, (ElectricalLink(('sphere','outer')),))
result = ConnectedShellBackend().solve(plan)
print(result.released_field_energy_j)  # 8.9875... J
```

`MixedRadialBackend` is an **exact spherical-symmetry analytical backend** for
the charge-free mixed geometry. Unlike a fake shell used in place of a solid
sphere, it has no phantom cavity or inner surface: the sphere's metal field
is zero, its inner-surface charge is zero, and its outer face carries its true
Gauss-law charge. It supplies `potential_at`, `electric_field_at`, measured-
by-formula conductor result records, and `surface_charge_density_at` for the
actual physical surfaces.

The same M14a charge-constraint matrix and exact `_solve_linear` are reused:
`ConnectionPlan` does not need a separate solver for every pair of connected
objects. Its `ConnectionResult` retains before/after conventional field
solutions, shell-by-name charge transfers, and an energy difference. The
existing field, conductor geometry, masks, density profiles, field-line
seeding, and standalone HTML viewer are reused without changes to its
rendering code or the FEMM compiler.

## Independent electrostatics and stored-energy identities

Let the innermost solid sphere radius be `a_0 = b_0 = R`, and surrounding
shells have inner and outer radii `a_i,b_i` with `b_i < a_(i+1)`.
The charge enclosed by the radial region immediately outside conductor `i` is

    T_i = sum_(j=0..i) Q_j.

Here the solid sphere outer-face charge is `T_0 = Q_0`, its nonexistent inner
face has zero charge, and the `i`th surrounding shell has inner/outer surface
charges `-T_(i-1)` and `T_i`. In each intervening vacuum gap,

    E(r) = k T_i / r^2  (outward), k = 1/(4 pi epsilon).

Inside any conductor `E=0`, at ideal metal surfaces `E` has different one-sided
limits, and outside the last shell `E=k T_last/r^2`. The potential of conductor
`i` relative to infinity is

    V_i = k [T_last/b_last + sum_(j=i..last-1) T_j*(1/b_j - 1/a_(j+1))].

Grounded conductor net charges are determined by imposing `V_i=0`. Connected
members share a potential and, for floating groups, conserve their combined
charge. For arbitrary valid named groups, these are **linear equations in the
unknown net charges**. The independent Gaussian elimination solves that
system once, and the ordinary radial solver verifies it afterward.

The electrostatic energy stored in the vacuum gaps and exterior is

    U = k/2 [sum_(i=0..last-1) T_i^2*(1/b_i - 1/a_(i+1))
             + T_last^2/b_last].

With no point sources in the solid+shell domain this is the **complete field
energy**. For the older all-shell domain with unchanged cavity point charges,
the fixed cavity-field contribution is omitted from both terms, but their
energy difference is still valid. `ConnectionResult.released_field_energy_j`
is the *nonnegative energy decrease* for the passive static connection under
the defined grounding/floating constraints. It may become heat in a real
resistive wire, but the code does **not** model current, resistivity, power,
contact physics or elapsed time.

## PDF validation anchors

**`02_conductors_half_size.pdf`, physical page 3, Single Correct Q27.**
A charged solid sphere radius **10 cm**, net **20 μC**, is surrounded by a
thin uncharged metal shell radius **20 cm**. They are joined by an ideal wire.
For an ideal zero-thickness shell the exterior field is unchanged while the
between-conductor field disappears. Thus

    U_before - U_after = k q^2/2 (1/0.10 - 1/0.20)
                       = 8.987551792... joules ≈ 9 J.

The expected multiple-choice option is **(2), 9 J**. The reproducible model
uses a `0.199999–0.200000 m` shell with tiny but nonzero thickness because
`SphericalShell` requires `inner_radius < outer_radius`. This introduces less
than 0.0001 J deviation and is called out in its HTML/JSON metadata rather
than claimed as exact zero-thickness geometry.

**Page 10, Linked Comprehension Q13–Q14.** Two **isolated thin shells**, of
radii 10 and 20 cm, are given equal and opposite charges with a maximum field
of `1.8×10^6 N/C` immediately outside the inner shell. Using Gauss's law,
`q = 4π ε R^2 Emax ≈ 2.00277 μC`; initial gap field energy is about
**0.09012 J** (the question's `0.09 J` choice). A floating link connects the
oppositely charged shells; their combined net charge is zero, so the final
radial field energy is zero. **All initial field energy is released** in the
ideal resistive-connection interpretation. This case intentionally reuses the
*old* M14a all-shell solver, not the new mixed solid+shell backend.

These examples and their answers are source anchored; numerical shell
thicknesses are tiny illustrative approximations to the printed ideal shells.

Acceptance requires Python 3.10/3.12, strict geometry and energy tests,
three or more independent charge-ground constraints, coordinate and parameter
covariance, both exported PDFs tested in Chromium for offline state switching,
and all retained **97 actual FEMM 5 regressions with zero skips**.

**No FEMM wiring claim:** the accepted numerical FEMM workflows verify
stability of existing solvers, not an explicit wire mesh or FEMM-computed
connection energy. Also excluded: separated nonconcentric connected spheres,
solid sphere with external point sources, dielectrics, dielectric interfaces,
finite wires, transient current or heating-rate trajectories, and the small
viewer boundary-colour polish deferred by the user.
