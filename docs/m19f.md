# M19f — Two verified prescribed-field scenarios, one honest timeline

## Educational motivation

M19d showed that a teacher can already explore multiple variations of one
physical arrangement by reusing immutable M19a field components. M19e added
source identities and physical world-axis labels. M19f connects **two existing
M19e viewer bundles** into one comparison, with an automatically derived
change ledger, same-metre-scale side-by-side source diagrams, a shared saved
time control, and labeled differences between **actually solved** states.

It is a bounded presentation/verification adapter, **not** a general scenario
registry, graph compiler, revision database, new electrostatic source law,
inverse solver, FEMM extension or second ODE integrator. Both constituent
viewers remain complete and individually usable. The baseline bundle and
the revised bundle are immutable, independently validated saved records.

## Authoring with existing M19e components

```python
from conductor_sim import (
    sample_prescribed_scenario_comparison, write_scenario_comparison,
)
from examples.ring_source_orientation import make_case, SOURCE_ANNOTATIONS

comparison = sample_prescribed_scenario_comparison(
    make_case('field-off'), make_case('axial-field'),
    source_annotations=SOURCE_ANNOTATIONS,
    labels=('External field off', 'External field along +x'),
    axis_orientation='horizontal', visual_frames=12,
)
write_scenario_comparison(comparison, 'output/m19f-rings.html')
```

The example's source geometry is derived from page 12, Q40–41 of
`02_conductors_half_size.pdf`, but the simulations are expressly **teacher
hypotheticals**. The original upward uniform field and symbolic particle
mass **do not** yield the assumed freely moving 1D axial trajectory. The
example instead uses 0.10 kg and compares no applied field with a +x field.
The original problem remains unchanged and is not passed off as simulated.

For two **already rendered** M19e viewer bundles use
`compare_prescribed_scenarios(left_bundle, right_bundle, ...)` without any
re-integration. The builder accepts independently saved
`TestChargeMotionResult`s to avoid even one repeated trajectory solve.

## Admissible comparisons

M19f rejects rather than invents an alignment when:

- Either input is not a separately valid, internally consistent M19a/M19e
  prescribed-field viewer bundle. It revalidates full time-series E/V/F,
  kinetic and potential energy, event/guard semantics and frame identity.
- The source IDs, inventory ordering or source **types** differ. IDs are
  supplied by the teacher and are not automatically inferred from charge or
  geometry. A source may change its charge, radius, position or field vector
  while retaining its authored ID.
- The two display planes have different origin, world orientation or
  **metre ranges**. A common physical scale is required for side-by-side
  spatial comparisons. The builder chooses the larger of the two real
  trajectory extents, without rotating any physics.
- The two lists of actually saved physical display-frame times differ. The
  common slider then selects only samples that exist in *both* sources. No
  interpolation of force, position, energy, geometry or time is performed.
- One source has an axis-only profile and the other a temporal field profile.

The comparison data are JSON-native and independently revalidated before
HTML serialization, including the derived change ledger and every saved
comparison. Rejected mutations include wrong source identity, unmatching
clock, changed plane, altered underlying force, false change claims and
fabricated delta values. This does not authenticate an arbitrarily replaced,
self-consistent numerical trajectory; independent physics/convergence tests
remain mandatory.

## What the teacher can inspect

The top card lists every actual change in source, probe or motion inputs,
including original and new values. Another line identifies the sources
whose **physical parameter dictionaries really remain identical**, plus
unchanged probe and motion input fields. Stable source IDs are not mistaken
for unchanged physical properties.

A master Play/Pause/Restart and time scrubber select exactly matching
elapsed-time frames in both existing embedded offline renderers. Two actual
source-geometry plots show the same world direction and same metre scale;
all original fixed-field, potential, force, velocity, motion and energy
charts remain available. A small table reports right-minus-left differences
in position norm, speed, kinetic energy and **gauge-dependent** potential
energy. Scenario-specific electrostatic potential references must be
considered before drawing conclusions about energy differences: qV across
inconsistent voltage zeros is **not** work between scenarios. Each scenario
must independently preserve its own correct mechanical energy convention.

The HTML is standalone and requires no network, browser-side physics or
second renderer implementation. The two embedded renderers retain their
individual controls. If a teacher moves one independently, using the
shared comparison slider resets both to a common saved time. At mobile
widths the two plots stack vertically rather than claiming simultaneity
by an invalid split view.

## Acceptance and boundaries

Require full Python 3.10 and 3.12 regressions, retained visualization and
legacy/browser/authoring workflows, the new M19f Python and *actual Chromium*
comparison acceptance, and all **97 real pinned FEMM 5 regressions with zero
skips**. The real FEMM tests demonstrate *previous conductor-domain*
non-regression, not FEMM-based verification of prescribed fixed-ring
trajectories. Independently compare representative M19a trajectories with
verified reference physics; the M19f change itself introduces no new laws.

No arbitrary-geometry off-axis ring/disc field is introduced. No automatic
teacher-model mutation, source text parsing, general constraint rewriting,
new physical identity registry, multi-moving-charge coupling, interaction of
hypothetical scenarios, future scene graph, inverse request, exact collision,
instantaneous field recomputation or graph interpolated dynamics is claimed.

This milestone is one incremental educational comparison, not evidence that
all arbitrary physical models can now be revised and compared.
