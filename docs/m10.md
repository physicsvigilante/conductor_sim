# M10: grounded infinite conducting plane

**Status:** M10a exact mirror-charge physics and M10b planar viewer implemented;
M10c FEMM 5 numerical validation **pending** on the review branch.

## Physical model and LLM authorability

`ConductingPlane(point_m, normal, constraint=Grounded(), name="plane")`
represents the **boundary of a conducting half-space**: all points with
`(x - point_m) · normal <= 0` are metal, with a strict zero-voltage condition
on the entire infinite boundary. `normal` points into vacuum, is automatically
normalized, and is independent of world coordinate orientation.

This is **not** a sheet with vacuum on both sides. The infinite conductor
cannot meaningfully have a prescribed finite *total* charge, so
`FixedNetCharge` is rejected at model construction. Existing spheres/shells
keep their APIs and solver capability limits unchanged.

Example (metres and coulombs):

```python
from conductor_sim import (ConductingPlane, ElectrostaticProblem,
                           Grounded, PointCharge, GroundedPlaneBackend)
problem = ElectrostaticProblem(
    (PointCharge(1e-9, (.02, -.03, .15)),),
    (ConductingPlane(point_m=(0,0,0), normal=(0,0,1),
                     constraint=Grounded()),),
)
solution = GroundedPlaneBackend().solve(problem)
print(solution.conductor_results)
print(solution.potential_at((.03, 0, .08)))
```

The **same problem** can be given to `Femm5PlaneBackend` for the currently
supported **one-source** case. No authored per-problem FEMM commands.

## Exact mirror-charge physics (M10a)

For each point charge `q_i` at height `h_i > 0`, reflect its position in the
plane, assign it a fictitious charge `-q_i`, and sum their fields **only in
vacuum**. The metal potential and field are zero. This exact analytical
reference supports 1–32 grouped active charges at arbitrary 3D locations on
the positive-normal side of *one* plane.

For a single charge of magnitude q at height h above the plane, the induced
surface-charge density at radial distance rho from its projection is:

`σ(rho) = -q h / [2π (rho² + h²)^(3/2)]`.

The total plane induced charge integrates to `-q`, the conductor voltage is
`0`, and the force is `F = -k q²/(4h²) * normal` with
`k = 1/(4π epsilon)`. Interaction energy of that charge with its induced
surface is `-k q²/(4h)` (the mirror-image interaction has a factor 1/2).
For multiple charges, the exact backend provides direct+image forces and the
associated consistent energy. Images are **not physical charges**.

## Shape-aware cross-section and rendering (M10b)

A dedicated geometry module represents both spherical and planar boundaries
using exact point-in-metal and segment-crossing operations. Existing
conductor-shell stop barriers and radius masks remain intact. A plane is drawn
as a **filled half-plane with a straight boundary**, not as a huge sphere.
Field lines terminate on the plane instead of entering metal, and contours
are masked there. Planar surface-density profiles use **signed distance in
metres along the displayed cross-section**, whereas spherical profiles retain
polar angle (degrees). Neither profile claims automatic 3D flux integration.

The viewer is an offline, self-contained export of **precomputed states**, not
a browser-time numerical solver or charge-motion simulation.

## Numerical FEMM 5 approximation (M10c)

FEMM 5 cannot mesh an infinite half-space. Its restricted backend builds a
**finite upper hemisphere** in `(r,z)` axisymmetric coordinates, choosing
`z=0` as a grounded planar conductor segment and creating a **separately
charged finite source sphere** at `r=0,z=h`. The quarter-circle outer
boundary approximates open space using a Robin condition suitable for the
leading dipole decay `V ∝ 1/r²`. The numerical domain radius and source core
radius are tunable for sensitivity checks.

The one actual physical conductor has prescribed `V=0`; its net charge
is **read from FEMM's plane-segment conductor property** and may differ from
`-q` due to finite-domain and discretization errors. Charge from the numerical
source is read separately. Surface-charge density is estimated from FEMM's
one-sided field slightly above the plane; it is **not** substituted from the
image solution or an exact FEMM surface-density integral.

Only **one nonzero point-charge site** is accepted numerically in M10. The
solver automatically chooses the charge's projection on the plane as its
symmetry-axis origin, so the authored plane and charge need not be aligned
with a world axis. Multiple or non-axisymmetric source arrangements are
explicitly rejected **before launching FEMM**, even though the analytical
backend can solve them.

## Validation and limitations

- Exact source reflection, conductor boundary condition, `E=-grad(V)`,
  source-force/energy gradient, signed surface density and integrated charge.
- Multiple non-collinear charges **analytically**, with exact Coulomb+image
  energy/force checks, rotation/translation covariance and unsupported-case
  refusals.
- Real FEMM: prescribed source charge, numerical conductor charge/potential,
  sampled electric fields and potential, local sigma and truncated sigma
  integral, mesh/core/outer-boundary sensitivity and world-coordinate rotation.
- A saved analytical and a separate **real FEMM generated** three-state HTML
  viewer must pass Chromium offline/controls/source-provenance checks.
- All previous M1–M9 and post-M9 numerical/visual regression tests are kept.

The finite FEMM source core and far boundary make this an **educational JEE
benchmark**, not a high-precision near-contact engineering solver. Exact
analytical results are valid only for an ideal infinite grounded half-space.
No general 3D FEMM, a charged isolated infinite plane, multiple grounded
planes, parallel plates, browser-time solving, hard FEMM process isolation,
scene graph, or charge trajectory is included in M10.
