# M3 — off-centre charge in a spherical conducting shell

**Status: accepted for one source position inside one spherical shell (2026-11-12).** See [numerical evidence](m3-acceptance.md).

## Authoring and geometry

`ElectrostaticProblem` remains the only authored problem object. An off-centre
`PointCharge(q, position_m=...)` strictly inside the spherical cavity is
accepted by `Femm5Backend`, with `FixedNetCharge(...)` or `Grounded()` applied
to the *whole* physical shell. A collection of coincident charges may be summed;
different charge positions are rejected, even if collinear. Multiple physical
conductors and arbitrary 3D arrangements remain unsupported.

For a single charge, **the center-to-charge vector is a symmetry axis**. The
compiler normalizes it to local +z; FEMM models `(r,z)` as a half-plane of
revolution. All world-coordinate potential queries and electric-field vectors
are transformed to/from this local frame. This permits source positions such
as `(0.04,0,0)` without any user-authored rotation or FEMM commands.

The point charge is approximated by a tiny *spherical conductor* with fixed
total source charge, centered at local `z=d`, where `d=|position-center|`.
The numerical radius is `min(a*source_radius_fraction, (a-d)/4)`, with `a` the
cavity radius. This is **not the exact point-charge solution for nonzero core
radius**: unlike the point source, an off-centre conducting sphere polarizes.
Sampling inside/on this artificial core is rejected. To establish numerical
accuracy, vary core radius and mesh parameters; compare against the image
charge solution at safe distances from the core.

The physical shell's inner and outer faces are assigned **one connected
conductor property**. The artificial source core is a *different* conductor
property and never appears in the authored physical conductor list. FEMM
independently solves the physical shell potential/total charge and source
charge. Gauss's law gives the *integrated* induced inner/outer charges from
those measured totals. These integrated totals are not local density maps.

## Independent analytical reference

`ImageChargeShellBackend` implements the exact Kelvin image solution for
one point charge at an arbitrary position inside a spherical shell. The
reference is independent of FEMM and may be used for testing, teaching, and
sampling:

- Spherical cavity radius `a`, physical shell outer radius `b`.
- Charge `q` at displacement `d<a` along the symmetry axis.
- Image charge `q' = -q*a/d` at `d' = a²/d` (outside the cavity).
- In the cavity: `V = V_shell + (q/|x-d| + q'/|x-d'|)/(4*pi*epsilon)`.
- For an isolated shell with total charge `Q_shell`, `V_shell=(q+Q_shell)/(4*pi*epsilon*b)`.
- For a grounded shell `V_shell=0`; the shell total charge must adjust to `-q`.
- The inner induced surface density at polar angle `theta` from +d is
  `sigma_inner = -q*(a²-d²)/(4*pi*a*(a²+d²-2*a*d*cos(theta))^(3/2))`.
- Outside a spherical shell, `sigma_outer=(q+Q_shell)/(4*pi*b²)` is uniform,
  with `Q_shell=-q` in the grounded case.

The `d=0` limit uses the regular centered-charge formulas to avoid numerical
cancellation. Source positions exactly on/outside the cavity are rejected.

## FEMM local surface charge estimates

`Femm5Solution.surface_charge_density("inner"|"outer", theta_rad,
 offset_fraction=0.02)` evaluates **FEMM's one-sided electric field near a
surface**, and multiplies by the permittivity and the correct outward-normal
sign. `theta_rad=0` points towards the displaced charge, `pi` away. The
inner normal of the **metal** points into the cavity. The method does not
return an analytical density, and it is not a direct FEMM boundary-element
surface-charge integral. Near the boundary, mesh/offset sensitivity matters.

One can numerically integrate `sigma(theta)*sin(theta)` over a sphere to
check the **independently sampled local** density against the inferred total
charge, and plot the density profile with a separate viewer later.

## Numerical acceptance gate (passed for the tested scope)

The dedicated GitHub workflow builds the pinned official FEMM 5 headless
solver and runs `tests/test_femm5_live.py` without skips. M3 tests must check:

1. Grounded and isolated shells: FEMM shell charge/potential, cavity, metal,
   and exterior potential/field against the independent image-charge solution.
2. The correct sign and pronounced **angular nonuniformity** of inner `sigma`;
   integrated near-surface `sigma` approximately accounts for the induced
   inner charge. For isolated shells the outer `sigma` stays approximately
   uniform; grounded exterior field vanishes.
3. Both positive and negative sources, world-coordinate rotations, and
   finite-core/mesh sensitivity, with the earlier M2a/M2b regressions intact.

The thresholds in test code are numerical acceptance tolerances, **not**
mathematical error bounds. Although M3 has passed, near-surface accuracy, small
clearance, arbitrary multi-charge fields and production process isolation
require further work. A procedural viewer and field-line tracing remain
separate milestones.
