# M13b — Reusable force-and-energy position explorer

## Reuse and authored problem contract

Build a normal immutable `ElectrostaticProblem` with a supported analytical
backend. Select **one authored charge index** and a distinct endpoint:

```python
from conductor_sim import PositionSweepSpec, sample_position_sweep, ParallelPlateBackend
from conductor_sim.visualization import write_visualization

# `problem` is an ordinary ElectrostaticProblem with its initial charge.
# No new solver or special-purpose viewer is written for a new arrangement.
spec = PositionSweepSpec(problem, charge_index=0, end_m=(0., 0., .15), steps=11)
bundle = sample_position_sweep(spec, ParallelPlateBackend(image_order=96),
                               resolution=25, line_steps=110)
write_visualization(bundle, 'output/force_energy_explorer.html')
```

`charge_index` identifies the **authored component**, not a lexicographically
sorted/grouped source index from an electrostatic solution. Matching on the
selected unique charge position and unchanged charge strength preserves its
identity when other sources change sort order. The sweep rejects zero-charge
selection, coincident/intersecting point sources, crossing conductor metal,
unsupported shell/cavity forces, unsupported geometries or directions, and
out-of-slice paths before a misleading plot is exported. The authored source
position is the starting point, and `end_m` determines the straight path.

Everything except that position remains fixed: source inventory and strengths,
conductor shapes, grounding/net-charge/fixed-voltage constraints and potential
gauge. `steps` permits 3–24 stored states, following the existing viewer limit.
A fixed cross-sectional slice is chosen automatically for planes and spheres,
or the author may supply a `SliceSpec` that actually contains the path.

## Data path and display

`sample_position_sweep` solves every state **once** with the caller's selected
backend; the shared M13a `force_energy(solution)` observations and the existing
`sample_frame` V/E/surface-density data consume that exact same solution.
Owned resources are closed even when force observations are unsupported or a
sampling step raises. No new numerical physics, back-end dispatch fallback,
scene graph, browser-side solver, or physical trajectory integrator is added.

Output uses the existing `conductor_sim.visualization.v1` schema with an
optional `position_sweep` object and one `force_energy` observation for each
matching frame. Earlier exports without that metadata remain supported.
The offline HTML uses the existing stored-state slider as **Charge position**
and provides:

* Selected-charge force arrow (in-slice projection, display-normalized length)
  and **physical total magnitude** in µN. The out-of-plane component is noted
  when it is appreciable; neither a projected arrow nor its pixel length is a
  quantitative force measurement.
* Two synchronized plots with fixed axes: **force component along the authored
  displacement** in µN against path coordinate in meters, and **mechanical
  interaction energy** in nJ against the same coordinate. Moving the slider
  moves both plot markers. All numeric data remain in SI in the JSON.
* External quasistatic work from the starting position, `U(s)-U(0)`, and work
  by the electrostatic force, its negative. A details disclosure shows direct,
  induced and background force/energy contributions from M13a.
* The existing field-line density control, potential colouring, source masks
  and conductor density plots. These do not alter the mechanical observations.

### Physical meaning

`F_s = F_total · unit(end-start)` and `F_s = -dU/ds` at fixed constraints.
For any two stored positions, `W_ext=ΔU` and `W_electric=-ΔU` when a charge
is moved sufficiently slowly to ignore kinetic-energy change. For fixed-voltage
capacitors this **mechanical interaction potential includes the work of the
voltage source**. It is not the divergent total field energy of infinite
plates. Original point self energies and conductor-only baseline terms are
omitted exactly as in M13a. Fixed-voltage changes, changing conductor shapes,
and changing charge inventory are not admissible work sweeps.

A charge between grounded plates has zero normal force at the geometric center
(up to series and roundoff error), but it is **unstable along the normal**.
The controller selects stored equilibrium snapshots; it is not elapsed time,
not a physical motion simulation, and not an assertion of dynamical stability.

## Boundary and verification

M13b is analytical-only: grounded plane, exterior grounded or fixed-Q sphere,
and parallel plates with grounded/fixed-voltage faces. Parallel-plate image
forces and energy are finite-order reference calculations, and image order
should be increased for convergence checks. FEMM 5 solutions do **not** offer
M13a force/energy observations; a sweep requesting them raises
`UnsupportedProblem` and closes its owned numerical solve, with no silent
analytical substitution. Non-collinear other charges may project out of the
cross-section, so only the force arrow's in-slice component is drawn; its full
vector remains in the stored data. Other cases needing 3D viewing are future
work.

Acceptance should require:

1. Independent grounded-plane force/energy formulas, sphere grounded/fixed-Q,
   grounded/bias two-plate tests, `F=-grad(U)` along the path and endpoint work.
2. Identity changes across lexicographic source reordering, non-intersection
   guards, axis/world-coordinate tests, validation of a single captured slice,
   and explicit FEMM-provider refusal and session ownership.
3. Browser testing that the same stored-state index controls the geometry,
   arrow and graph markers; that field density never alters force, and that
   older viewer exports are unchanged. Assert no JS errors, network requests,
   or narrow-mobile overflow.
4. Pinned real FEMM 5 regressions with zero skips, plus new refusal coverage.

No M13c open-ended JEE authoring trials, time integration, collision response,
velocity, kinetic energy, particle mass or trajectories are claimed by M13b.
